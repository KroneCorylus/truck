# R11: two-edge fillets and corner chamfers

Status: implemented in the kernel working tree, 2026-09-08. Application selection
resolution, dependency pinning and API wiring remain downstream work.

## Reproduction

An isolated checkout of the exact pinned revision `9f0612fff6225a33d9ebdfa50d11834908d27bcc`
reproduced the unsupported two-edge fillet on the normalized, swept `[0,10]³` box.
Single-edge fillet/chamfer and three-edge fillet controls succeeded. Sequential
two- and three-edge chamfers failed on their second call, in both selection orders.
Each remaining edge was resolved geometrically against the modified solid by its
original midpoint, so these failures do not depend on application reference resolution.
The separate generic error in the original screenshot is not independently diagnosed.

`r11_corners.rs::sequential_chamfers_reproduce_kernel_gap` retains this reproduction.
The existing single-edge and closed tangent-wire chamfer APIs have no contract for
perpendicular or branching selections; the new selected-set API handles them together.

## Calling contract and corner geometry

Use original edge IDs resolved against the input modeling solid:

```rust,ignore
use truck_shapeops::fillet::{try_fillet_solid_edges, try_chamfer_solid_edges};
let rounded = try_fillet_solid_edges(&solid, &[ex, ey], 1.0, 0.001)?;
let two_bevels = try_chamfer_solid_edges(&solid, &[ex, ey], 0.2, 0.001)?;
let three_bevels = try_chamfer_solid_edges(&solid, &[ex, ey, ez], 0.2, 0.001)?;
```

The corresponding APIs without `try_` return `Option<BlendResult>`. Each call returns
an ordinary `truck_modeling::Solid`, modified-face correspondence and generated faces.
Modified faces are in input order; generated strips/bevels follow the shell's original
edge traversal, followed by any spherical corner faces. Selection-list order does not
set generated-face order. Use the modified-face correspondences and generated-face
adjacency for naming, rather than persisting process-local IDs or result indices.
Empty selections preserve the solid and return empty history.

Let `w = 10 - z`, with the selected corner at `(x,y,w) = (0,0,0)`.

- Two fillets of radius `r` are exact rational cylindrical patches meeting in the
  plane `x = y`. Their common elliptical arc is
  `x = y = r(1 - cos θ), w = r(1 - sin θ)`, `0 ≤ θ ≤ π/2`.
  It runs from `(0,0,r)` on Ez to `(r,r,0)` on the top face. Each strip remains tangent
  to its two supporting planes. **The miter seam is a crease**, not a smooth spherical
  transition between the strips. There is no additional corner face. Ez retains its
  original straight intersection of two planes, shortened by `r`.
- Two equal-distance chamfers intersect the original solid with `y+w ≥ d` and
  `x+w ≥ d`. Their common straight seam joins `(0,0,d)` and `(d,d,0)`.
  Ez is shortened by `d`, with no bevel along its remaining length.
- Selecting all three chamfer edges additionally imposes `x+y ≥ d`. The three bevel
  faces meet at `(d/2,d/2,d/2)`, with **no additional triangular corner face**. Each pair
  shares a straight edge from this point to its contact on an original face.

Chamfers use exact planes and lines, with all bevel half-spaces intersected together.
The shared convex-shell validation checks straightness, planarity and outward convexity.
Fillet miter curves are affine projections of rational circular sections along their
cylinder axes; ruled patches between these sections remain exact cylinders.
No input nudges, additional selected edges, display-mesh geometry or sequential feature
workarounds are involved.

Two-edge fillet junctions currently require orthogonal trihedral corners. The existing
three-edge spherical and collinear subdivision cases remain supported. Chamfer selections
require a convex planar shell with three edges at every vertex and one equal distance
on both sides of every selected edge. Asymmetric set chamfers are outside this API;
existing asymmetric single-edge and tangent-wire APIs are unchanged. Concave/curved
corners and collisions with other solid boundaries remain outside the contract.

Diagnostic variants distinguish `UnsupportedTopology`, `UnsupportedGeometry` and
`NonPlanarFace` from `OutsideNeighbour` for sizes that leave the available faces or
collapse required edges/contacts. Nonpositive/nonfinite sizes return `InvalidParameter`.
Invalid tolerances, duplicate IDs and unknown IDs retain their specific diagnostics.
All failures preserve the input. Geometric validation is sample based, not a certified
global intersection proof for arbitrary geometry.

## Independent volume references and validation

For length `L = 10`, radius `r` and distance `d`, a single fillet removes
`L r²(1 - π/4)`; a single chamfer removes `L d²/2`.
Two fillets add back overlap `r³(5/3 - π/2)`, obtained by integrating
`[r - sqrt(r² - (r-w)²)]²` from `w=0` to `r`. The three-edge spherical construction
adds back `(2 - 7π/12)r³`. Two chamfers add back `d³/3`; three add back
`3d³/4` by inclusion-exclusion (three pair overlaps `d³/3`, triple overlap `d³/4`).

| Selection | Fillet, r = 1: volume mm³ | Chamfer, d = 0.2: volume mm³ |
|---|---:|---:|

| Ex, Ey | 995.803833608 | 999.602666667 |
| Ex, Ey, Ez | 993.729349187 | 999.406 |

The regression constructs exact straight square edges, attaches a plane and sweeps it
10 mm. It resolves selections by endpoints and repeats the table after reversing the
selection and after a translation plus an arbitrary-axis rotation. Reversed calls
produce identical compressed geometry/topology. Tests check closed oriented topology,
Euler characteristic, closed welded tessellation, nonzero face areas, boundary/surface
agreement, outward supporting half-spaces, miter equations, tangent planar contacts,
unselected-edge preservation, complete face history and unchanged inputs. Additional
cases join miters at both ends of a strip and mix them with a spherical corner.

Mesh tolerance is `0.001` mm. The volume allowance is curved area times mesh tolerance,
with a `1e-6` mm³ rounding floor; planar chamfers use only that rounding floor.
Each new corner case undergoes a boolean at `x=5` and another through the actual junction
(`z=9.5` for the fillet, `z=9.9` for chamfers). The expected clipped volumes come from
the same cross-section integrals and inclusion-exclusion, independently of construction.
The corner and the cut-through-corner result undergo prepared STEP export/import at
`0.00005` mm fitting tolerance. Imported topology and robust mesh closure are checked,
with volume allowance `area × (0.001 + 2 × 0.00005)`. Export and booleans leave the
original modeling geometry unchanged.
